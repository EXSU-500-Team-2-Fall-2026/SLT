## How to complete each appraisal

1. Read the full text and relevant supplements. Record complete authors, publication year, venue, DOI, and publication status in the final bibliography; use the published version when available. Do not count a preprint and its journal version as two references.
2. Extract sample sizes with their units (patients, lesions, or images), class balance, reference standard, modality, and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Distinguish binary malignancy, melanoma-only, multiclass diagnosis, segmentation, and management tasks.
3. Record whether splits are patient-, lesion-, or image-level; whether tuning, calibration, and threshold selection are separate from final evaluation; and whether external cohorts overlap development sources.
4. Extract AUROC, AUPRC, sensitivity/specificity at the stated threshold, and calibration measures when reported. Record confidence intervals or repeated-run variability, and the exact table, figure, or page supporting each result. Do not infer missing metrics or compare headline scores across different cohorts as if they were the same experiment.
5. Write a short appraisal: what the paper supports, what it cannot establish, and the specific consequence for our design. Preserve counter-evidence and negative findings.
6. A second team member should check every numerical claim selected for the manuscript against the source. Only mark a paper team-read when a named member has read it.

## Planned synthesis

- **Clinical motivation and intended use:** P15 and P24–P26. Connect reader assistance and prospective evaluations to the limits of retrospective classification and referral claims.
- **Dataset integrity and evaluation safeguards:** P01–P02 and P06–P08. Distinguish official source records, duplicate audits, modality-specific leakage evidence, and general methodological guidance.
- **Model comparison and information sources:** P03–P05, P09–P11, P14, and P20–P21. Explain metadata, handcrafted features, learned representations, imbalance handling, and the limits of comparisons across different inputs and cohorts.
- **Calibration and uncertainty:** P13, P16, and P19, with P12 as secondary background. Distinguish ground-truth uncertainty, predictive uncertainty, performance confidence intervals, and probability calibration. Our selected future method is logistic recalibration; P16 provides calibration context and temperature scaling evidence, not direct validation of our specific protocol.
- **Generalization, representation, and coverage:** P17–P18 and P22–P28. Discuss dataset shift, skin-tone representation, modality differences, prospective evaluation, and broader diagnostic coverage.

The final review should connect findings into an argument rather than summarize one paper per paragraph. Preserve negative findings and avoid comparing headline scores from different tasks as if they were evaluated on the same cohort. Official repositories and reviews support the review but do not replace predominantly recent primary research. Paper IDs are internal evidence-table identifiers, not manuscript reference numbers.
